Recommendation logic

Why partial shift?

For the selected campaign and market, the example favors acting on the lift signal while retaining room to learn.

1
Compare caused impact, not platform credit alone.

Search has the highest reported ROAS in this fictional example, but social and programmatic have higher estimated incremental ROAS. Incremental estimates ask which sales were added by the advertising.

2
Account for uncertainty.

The channel ranges are broad and overlap. That means the evidence does not establish a certain winner; a full shift would put more budget behind a still-maturing estimate.

3
Balance opportunity with learning time.

A partial move acts during the time-sensitive campaign while preserving some spend in the current mix. The example moves half of the flexible amount, then reassesses after channel learning windows.

4
Respect execution limits and business outcomes.

The proposal stays within the editable budget, keeps CTV commitments and creator notice periods intact, and uses incremental profit when margin data is available. Without reliable margin, incremental revenue is only a proxy.

The suggestion should change if new outcome data separates the ranges, delivery changes materially, constraints shift, or the business objective changes. These are example inputs, not LiftLab production logic.
